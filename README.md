# Dissemination of Alabuga Start Promotional Propaganda
By Zecharias Zelalem, for The Continent newspaper (www.thecontinent.org).

This dataset catalogues the mass media dissemination campaign promoting the controversial Russian state backed Alabuga Start recruitment initiative. Launched in 2022 in the wake of Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, the Alabuga Start program seeks to recruit young women from Africa and across the developing world to work at a drone factory installation in Russia’s Tatarstan region. The program is depicted by its directors as a vocational schooling and work opportunity, offering high paying jobs as café baristas or in hotel management among other positions, for young women aged 18-22. But media investigations and research have established that recruits are lured to Tatarstan under false pretenses to work in drone factories. The program is an extension of the Russian war machine, and its directors have been sanctioned by various European governments for its involvement with the war in Ukraine. The program’s directors have been accused of migrant exploitation and human trafficking among other crimes.
   The media organizations listed in this database serve audiences across the developing world in Africa, the Americas, the Indian subcontinent, former Soviet republics and to a lesser extent, the Middle East. They are a total of 117 newsrooms and one national journalism association. The articles published promote the Alabuga Start program, downplay the dangers, dismiss legitimate reporting as fabrications and feature glowing testimony about it. Virtually none of them mention the fact that women are expected to work at a drone factory, which has been targeted in Ukrainian aerial attacks that have left African recruits injured.
  The grand majority of the articles follow a preset template, meaning that they aren’t produced by the newsrooms. The investigation by The Continent revealed that staff from Alabuga Start authored many of the scripts, before they were circulated and recirculated by news platforms across the world, with no scrutiny. This meant the same articles were published and republished often word for word, dozens of times. Some scripts were circulated and republished in media across dozens of countries and multiple continents. A number of the media outlets listed the articles’ placement as having been sponsored without disclosing who the sponsor was. A tiny few outlets were fully transparent about who produced the work, crediting the Alabuga Start program as the producer. Over 90% of the articles were published with no disclosures made whatsoever.
    This initiative was the backbone of an investigation published in November 2026 by The Continent newspaper, exposing the Russian state’s weaponization of the credibility, acceptance and prominence enjoyed by leading newsrooms, to whitewash the reputation of a program linked to the endangerment of young vulnerable migrant women across Africa and the developing world  The data is non-exhaustive.  **OSINT Limitations:** 
 
 This data is based on open-source research across a diverse array of global sources and languages. Because media transparency varies widely by jurisdiction, this information is provided as is without guarantees of absolute accuracy or completeness. Errors or omissions may exist. Because promotional and sponsored articles are frequently deleted or altered post-publication, every entry includes redundant archiving links (Proton Drive, Wayback Machine, Archive.ph, and/or Ghostarchive) to ensure verifiability.
